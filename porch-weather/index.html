    Every hour gets a score out of 100 — shown on its pill, and beside the verdict up top — and the score picks the tier. The number is the nuance: a 73 and a 59 are both "OK", but only one of them is nearly good.

    Each hour shows both readings: the feels-like temperature, which is what the rating is built on, and the actual air temperature beside it. They differ when wind chill or humidity is doing something to the air.

    Each hour carries an icon for what the sky is doing — sun, moon, cloud, fog, drizzle, rain, snow, thunderstorm — and the five chips are keyed to their factor: thermometer, droplet (humidity), wind, rain, cloud, haze (air quality). In the week grid, a block picks up a small marker when rain, snow or a thunderstorm is expected, or when air quality, wind or humidity is the thing ruining that hour. Air quality comes from Open-Meteo's air-quality service as the US AQI; if that request fails the chip reads "—" and the hour is scored on the other five factors.

    The score is a weighted blend of the six factors, and then one rule applies: an hour can score no more than 18 points above its worst factor. So a thunderstorm (which scores about 5) drags the hour to 23 — Nope — however pleasant the temperature is, while an afternoon that's merely a bit muggy (65) can still reach 83 and read Good. Comfort bands shift a few degrees with your temperature setting, and after dark cloud cover stops counting.
